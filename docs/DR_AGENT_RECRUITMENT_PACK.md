## 1. Top 7 Places to Find DR Agents Online

### 1. AEI — Asociación de Agentes y Empresas Inmobiliarias
**URL:** [aei.com.do/directorio/agentes](https://aei.com.do/directorio/agentes)

**What it covers:** The national professional association's public member directory. Approximately 1,464 registered agents and 229 agencies — the largest verified roster in the country. Each card shows agent name, city, phone, and AEI registration number.

**Why it is a good source:** AEI members pay annual dues and have completed at minimum a basic certification. They are professionals, not hobbyists. Because AEI requires renewal, inactive agents age out.

**How to filter to solo practitioners:** The directory has city filters. Set city to Santo Domingo, Santiago, or Punta Cana. Look for agents listed without a company affiliation (their company field will be blank or list their own name). Avoid entries where the company field names a franchise (RE/MAX, Century 21, Coldwell Banker) — those agents answer to a broker and have less personal pressure to build their own marketing presence, which is the pain point AHO solves.

---

### 2. DRListings.com — Agent Directory
**URL:** [drlistings.com/agents](https://www.drlistings.com/agents/)

**What it covers:** An English-language MLS-style portal running since 2006. It lists ~48 profiled individual agents and separate broker pages. Agent profiles include photo, bio, city, listings count, and a direct contact link.

**Why it is a good source:** Agents who self-list on DRListings are actively marketing to international buyers (US diaspora, expats, investors). They already understand that their listings need online visibility — they are pre-qualified on the pain AHO solves. The listings volume shown on each profile tells you how active they are right now.

**How to filter to solo practitioners:** Read the bio. Agents who write in first person ("I specialize in…") and list fewer than 10 active properties are likely independent. Skip profiles where the bio reads as a company description or mentions a team.

### 3. Properstar — DR Agent Pages
**URL:** [properstar.com/dominican-republic/real-estate-agents](https://www.properstar.com/dominican-republic/real-estate-agents)

**What it covers:** 858 agents listed nationally; 415 in Santo Domingo alone, 161 in Santo Domingo Este. Profiles include specialty tags (luxury, rentals, investment), languages spoken, and active listing count.

**Why it is a good source:** Properstar syndicates from multiple Latin American portals and surfaces agents who are actively posting listings. The listing count per agent is visible on the card before you click in — a fast signal for activity level.

**How to filter to solo practitioners:** Sort by city. Filter for agents with 3-15 active listings (enough to be active, few enough that they are not a full agency). Agents with a personal photo rather than a logo are almost always independent. Properstar also shows languages spoken — prioritize Spanish + English bilingual agents, as they are likely serving the expat/investor segment where AHO's English UI is an asset.

### 4. Facebook Group — Agentes Inmobiliarios en República Dominicana
**URL:** [facebook.com/groups/agentesinmobiliariosenrepublicadominicana](https://www.facebook.com/groups/agentesinmobiliariosenrepublicadominicana/)

**What it covers:** A peer group where active agents post listings, ask questions about transactions, and share industry news. Members are overwhelmingly independent and small-agency practitioners — RE/MAX and Century 21 have corporate channels for this.

**Why it is a good source:** You can watch the group for 15 minutes and immediately identify the most active posters. Anyone posting listings in the last 30 days is provably active. You can comment on their post publicly ("Que bonito proyecto — le escribo por DM") and then message them, which is warmer than a cold DM.

**How to filter to solo practitioners:** Look at the poster's profile. If they have a personal account (not a business page) and are posting individual listings with their personal WhatsApp, they are almost certainly solo or very small. Avoid posts that say "nuestra cartera de propiedades" (our portfolio) — that is an agency account.

### 5. Instagram Hashtags — Active Solo Agents
**Key hashtags to search:**
- `#agenteinmobiliariord`
- `#inmobiliariaRD`
- `#bienesraicesRD`
- `#propiedadesRD`
- `#santodomingorealestate`

**Why it is a good source:** Dominican agents are highly active on Instagram for listing promotion. Solo practitioners post their own face alongside listings — this is the differentiator from agency accounts. The most recent posts show who is active today, not a year ago.

**How to filter to solo practitioners:** Look for personal accounts (not business pages) with 500-5,000 followers. Accounts in this range are typically one-person operations who are grinding for reach — exactly the people who will appreciate a tool that does social posting in 60 seconds. Accounts over 20K followers are usually agencies or teams with marketing support already.

**Watch for:** The AEI Instagram account [@aeiorg](https://www.instagram.com/aeiorg/?hl=es) has 22K followers and often features individual member agents in their posts. Following AEI's feed and clicking through to the tagged agents in their member spotlights is a fast path to vetted solo practitioners.

**What it covers:** LinkedIn surfaces DR agents who are actively using the platform for professional visibility. A significant subset of DR agents on LinkedIn are bilingual and serve the investor/expat market — slightly higher-end than the Facebook group audience, but very relevant.

**Why it is a good source:** LinkedIn gives you three things the other channels do not: (a) how long they have been in real estate (years in role visible on profile), (b) education and prior career, which tells you if they are a career switcher still building their book, and (c) mutual connections with Michał — a mutual connection makes an InMail dramatically warmer.

Profiles with a generic title like "Real Estate" without a named company are often solo operators. Avoid profiles where the current employer is a franchise.

### 7. El Inmobiliario — "Conoce tu Agente" Editorial Series
**URL:** [inmobiliario.do/category/mercado-inmobiliario/conoce-tu-agente](https://inmobiliario.do/category/mercado-inmobiliario/conoce-tu-agente/)

**What it covers:** El Inmobiliario is the DR's first real estate media publication. Their "Conoce tu Agente" (Meet Your Agent) section is an editorial series profiling individual agents — written interviews with backstory, specialty, and philosophy. These agents have agreed to be profiled publicly, which means they are actively building their personal brand.

**Why it is a good source:** These are not random directory entries. Each agent has a detailed article with their specialty, market focus, and personal motivation. The personalization hook writes itself — you can open your message referencing something specific from their published interview.

**How to filter to solo practitioners:** The series tends to feature independent agents and small-shop owners rather than franchise agents (who have corporate PR). Every profile is a warm outreach opportunity.

## 2. The Pitch Template

### Core pitch (base text, personalize before sending)

**Spanish (use this — Dominican market default):**

> Hola [Nombre], vi [hecho específico de su perfil/publicación]. Construí una herramienta que convierte el enlace de un apartamento en 9 textos de anuncio y 3 gráficas en 60 segundos — listos para publicar en Facebook e Instagram. Estoy reclutando 3 o 4 agentes en la República Dominicana para una beta privada con una tarifa de fundador permanente. No le pido nada hoy — si le interesa ver cómo funciona, con gusto le envío el enlace.
>
> — Michał Babula, advertisehomes.online

**English (for bilingual agents or expat-specialist agents):**

> Hi [Name], I came across [specific fact about their profile/listing]. I built a tool that turns a listing URL into 9 ad captions and 3 graphics in 60 seconds — ready to post on Facebook and Instagram. I'm recruiting 3-4 agents in the Dominican Republic for a private beta with a permanently discounted founder rate. No ask today — if you want to see how it works, I'm happy to send you the link.
>
> — Michał Babula, advertisehomes.online

**Word count:** Spanish version: 93 words. English version: 85 words. Both inside the 80-120 target.

**The personalization rule:** Replace `[hecho específico de su perfil/publicación]` with one concrete detail. Do not write "your impressive work." Write "su apartamento en Evaristo Morales que publicó el lunes" or "su perfil en AEI donde indica que se especializa en proyectos turísticos." One specific beats ten compliments.

## 4. "First 10 Minutes" Checklist

Follow this exactly the first time you sit down. Do not improvise the order.

**Step 1 — Open AEI's agent directory (2 min)**
Go to [aei.com.do/directorio/agentes](https://aei.com.do/directorio/agentes). Filter to Santo Domingo. Scroll past any entry where the company field shows RE/MAX, Century 21, or Coldwell Banker. Open the first 10 entries that look like individuals in separate tabs.

**Step 2 — Score each agent against 3 criteria (3 min)**
For each tab, check: (a) Do they have a photo, not a logo? (b) Is their WhatsApp number visible? (c) Is there any indication of recent activity — a listing, an Instagram link, a website? Keep the 5 that check 2 of 3. Close the rest.

**Step 3 — Find one specific fact per agent (3 min)**
For each of your 5, do a 30-second Google search: their name + "República Dominicana." You are looking for one of: a recent listing you can name, a specialty you can reference, or a quote from an interview. Write it in a note. If you find nothing in 30 seconds, skip that agent and go to the next one in the directory.

**Step 4 — Personalize the WhatsApp opener and send (3 min, one per agent)**
Open the WhatsApp template above. Replace [Nombre] with their first name. Replace the generic reference with the specific fact you found. Send as three separate messages. Do not copy-paste the same text to all five — rewrite message 1 each time with the one fact. This takes 90 seconds per agent and is the only thing that separates a response from silence.

**Step 5 — Log each send in a simple note (1 min)**
In a note app or a sheet, write: agent name / channel used / date sent / specific fact used. You need this when they reply and you want to continue the conversation without sounding like you forgot who they are. No CRM required — a plain text note works.

Total elapsed time: approximately 12 minutes. Then wait 48 hours before following up on anyone who has not replied.

## Market Notes

**Language:** Pitch in Spanish by default for every cold first contact in DR. Switch to English only if (a) their profile is in English, (b) they are visibly targeting US/expat buyers, or (c) they respond to you in English. Using Spanish first signals respect for the market; using English first signals you think the DR is a foreign market you are selling into.

**WhatsApp is the primary business communication channel in DR.** Email open rates are lower than in North America or Europe. LinkedIn is growing but still secondary. If you have a WhatsApp number, use it first. Email is your fallback, not your primary.

**The solo practitioner pain is real and visible.** DR agents post their listings manually to Facebook and Instagram — often the same photo with the same caption copy-pasted across multiple groups. The 60-second automation pitch maps directly onto a task they do every day and find tedious. You do not need to educate them on the problem; you need to show them the solution exists.

**Franchise agents (RE/MAX, Century 21) are not the wedge ICP.** They have corporate marketing support, brand templates, and broker oversight. They are not in pain about social content. Target them after you have proof cases from independents.

**AEI membership is a trust signal for the agent, not a gating requirement for AHO.** You do not need to restrict beta invites to AEI members. But reaching out to AEI members signals to those agents that you did your homework — referencing their AEI registration number in an outreach message would be unusual and memorable.
